Predictive models: who will not come, and why

Task

  • Who: score every invitation by its chance of a screening.
  • Why: the drivers and eight explainable segments.
  • What to do and how to verify it: Interventions & tests.

Evaluation

  • Label: screened within 12 months of the invitation (all numbers on this page).
  • Split by woman, 70 / 15 / 15; numbers are on the held-out test women.
  • ROC AUC: chance that a screened invitation scores above a non-screened one.

Source

  • Models: training/classifiers (explain, ablation), training/per_group_experts.
  • Segments: segmentation/segments.py.
  • Built by build_classifiers.py.

The models

Eight model families that score every invitation on its own, from a single readable tree to a neural network, plus one sequence model that reads the woman's invitations in order. All use the same 19 attributes of the invitation and the woman's history. Train vs. test ROC AUC shows how much each one memorises.

Sequence model: does the woman's whole history help? (test ROC AUC, 12 months)

Earlier outcomes are fed only once their 3, 6 or 12-month window had closed. 38% of women have a single invitation in the data and at most 5. No bootstrap interval yet for the GRU.

    Caveats

    • Observational data: the drivers and segments describe who comes, not what makes her come. Only the experiment shows the effect of an intervention.
    • The 12-month label of invitations from late 2024 may be incomplete; 2020 is lower because of COVID (see exploration).
    • Women aged 40–44 appear only as first invitations, so age and first invitation are confounded.
    • Response by region and district is in the regional report; location shifts the level, not the drivers.
