Interventions and tests: what to do, and how to know it worked

Why women miss screening

A missed examination can reflect a belief, a difficult experience, an access problem or an inaccurate record. A non-response alone cannot tell which. [3][9]

Keep the denominators straight

Czech insurer invitations target women already overdue. Their response rate is not the same as coverage among all eligible women. [1][18]

Coverage is a separate measure: 59.9%. Our calculation from the public Czech dataset for women aged 45–69 in 2024: 1,111,008 examined of 1,856,010, using the dataset's two-year coverage definition. [16]

Nine tests, matched to the next action

Each design specifies the service being offered and its comparator. All nine are proposals for Czech evaluation; a trial estimates the offered intervention's effect, not, by itself, the psychological mechanism. Women may have several barriers at once.

Test the offer, not the women who accept it. Nanda's scheduled-versus-unscheduled subgroups were not randomized. The three-arm design below separates the added offer of immediate booking from reminder calling. [29] Luckmann reported US$30.45 per additional mammogram for reminder calls versus letters; collect Czech staffing and service costs rather than importing that estimate. [30]

Two different reminder questions

No appointment yet? Test a reminder to book. The US trials excluded women with an appointment already scheduled and found +2.1 and +2.5 percentage points in three-month completion from a sequence of texts. [10]

Appointment already scheduled? Test a reminder to attend. The English +5.3-point result was for an SMS 48 hours before an assigned appointment. [6]

These effects have different populations, comparators and outcomes. Do not pool them or use either as a forecast for the other stage. A reminder effect does not establish forgetting as the cause of non-attendance.

04 How to verify: one shared protocol

Every test follows the same protocol, so results can be compared and the segments can be targeted next round.

Steps

  1. Population. Verify eligibility and prior mammograms before allocation; route women with symptoms to clinical care. Assign each invitation its segment with the rules (no model needed). [18]
  2. Allocation. Randomise by woman: 1:1 for two-arm trials, 1:1:1 for the calling trial. Stratify by centre and prior attendance, and by invitation segment where applicable; keep her in her arm on later invitations. Preserve current care, including routine appointment reminders, in all arms. [19]
  3. Primary outcome. Most invitation-stage trials use claims-confirmed mammography within 12 months of randomization, with early reads at 3 and 6 months. The calling trial prespecifies completion at 90 days as primary and 180 days secondary. The reminder-to-attend trial uses index-appointment attendance, with 60-day completion secondary; record correction uses verified corrections within 90 days. Each needs its own baseline and reference date. [29][6][16]
  4. Size. Fix the smallest worthwhile improvement, power and number of comparisons in advance; size each arm with the planner below.
  5. Analysis. Intention to treat; absolute percentage-point difference with a 95% interval, relative effect, cost per additional screened woman, complaints and equity comparisons. [4][19]
  6. Decide and re-target. Keep, change or drop the intervention per segment; use the model score to prioritise within a segment next round, not as evidence of why women do not come.

Decide the question first

Pre-register the outcome, follow-up, minimum worthwhile improvement, sample size, stopping rule and analysis. Keep women in their group even if they never read the message, and handle missing outcomes explicitly. [19]

A booking is a step, not the endpoint

Beauchamp measured bookings, whereas Nanda and Hegenscheid measured actual screening. The Australian result alone cannot establish completion, but the other trials supply direct attendance evidence. Track contact, booking and attendance separately. [11][29][31]

Know what a null result means

In Singapore, adding rewards, videos or a hotline to reminders did not significantly improve uptake. A null result is useful; it is not proof that two approaches are equivalent. [13]

Respect the unit of change

An intervention that changes a whole centre or practice (opening hours, a doctor's recommendation) needs centres or practices randomised, clustering in the analysis and a separate sample-size calculation. Prevent women receiving conflicting variants across overlapping trials. [20]

How many women a test needs

Pick a segment to start from its invitation response, then set the smallest improvement worth detecting. For reminder and calling trials, first measure the eligible cohort's baseline and volume at that stage. This planner sizes one pair of arms; it does not give the total size of the three-arm calling study. Size its C−A and C−B contrasts separately, then finalize the joint design.

Improve the system around the letter

Recommendations derived from the evidence, with what our data adds. Their order is a proposed implementation sequence, not a ranking of effectiveness.

Suggested rollout

  1. Fix the denominator. Stop letters to women screened less than two years ago (segment A); validate overdue status, contact details, outcome capture and capacity; interview a sample of non-attendees.
  2. Run focused pilots. Prioritize proactive calling with booking help for verified-unbooked postal nonresponders, using the three-arm design. Candidate segments include C, D, E, G and H; segment membership is not evidence of treatment benefit. Test SMS booking reminders and clinician endorsement separately; evaluate appointment reminders from centre booking lists. [29][31]
  3. Scale selectively. Decide on completed visits, uncertainty, cost, experience and equity; check repeat attendance at the next eligible round.
